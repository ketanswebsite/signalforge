| 20 | MEDIUM | The monthly AI sweep re-scored next to nothing. `runConvictionSweep()` scores through `getConviction()`, which serves any stored verdict up to 36 days old without computing — and first Saturdays are 28 or 35 days apart, so on sweep morning every verdict from the last sweep is still inside that window. The sweep counted each one as "scored". Seen on production 2026-09-19: after the 2026-09-05 sweep had run its whole queue, 257 symbols still held a newest verdict dated 08-15…08-22 — older than the 14-day resume cut-off, so by design they had to be re-scored (2,030 skipped + 257 handed back + 2,737 never-scored symbols computed = the 5,029 universe). No verdict older than the 37-day window was ever served — the gate stayed inside what the owner accepted — which is why nothing looked wrong. | **Closed 2026-09-19:** the sweep asks the engine for `fresh` verdicts (`getConviction({ fresh: true })`: past the memory cache and the stored verdict, in-flight dedup kept, today's row replaced instead of ignored; a fresh re-score that finds every source down leaves the stored verdict in charge). The sweep's tally is honest now — `scored` / `reused` / `blind` — and `GET /api/ops/conviction-stats` shows the same from the table: a genuine sweep is a universe-sized spike on one date. **Cost:** this is the ~5,029 Gemini calls a month the monthly cadence was always meant to cost; the bug was averaging about half of that, so the actual bill roughly doubles. `CONVICTION_SWEEP_FRESH=false` is the old behaviour exactly. |
